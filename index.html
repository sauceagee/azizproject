The idea

Each winter, hundreds of millions of monarchs collapse onto a few hectares of oyamel fir in the mountains of Michoacán. Below roughly ten degrees they enter torpor: they cannot fly, cannot crawl, cannot leave. They hang in clumps so dense the branches bend, each one gripping the bark or the butterfly above it.

Torpor is not sleep. It is a wager — every gram of stored fat has to last until March. Warmth is what breaks the wager. Sunlight, a hand, the heat of a body standing too close. A butterfly roused in December spends what it cannot replace, and one that drops to the forest floor usually does not climb back.

This is why visitors to the sanctuaries are asked to stand still, to stay quiet, to keep to the path. This piece puts you on a path you do not control, and leaves you only your hands.

Why

I wanted an interaction that punishes attention. Most interactive work rewards you for pressing further in — for looking harder, getting closer, touching the thing. Here the reward and the damage are the same gesture. You cannot reach the colony without warming it, and you cannot warm it without spending something that belonged to spring.

You are carried from branch to branch whether you like it or not. There is no walking away and no way to be told off. The only thing you can decide is whether to keep your hands down. Fragility is not a defence, and the trace you leave does not need your consent to be permanent.
